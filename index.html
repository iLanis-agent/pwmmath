PWM Math

A 5 V rail at 30% duty averages 1.5 V.

Boundary: arithmetic only PWM Math works out average voltages, duty cycles, frequencies and counter resolutions from the numbers you type, treating the waveform as an ideal square wave between zero and the rail. It has not seen your signal and does not know the rise time, the ringing, the load or the timer mode, and it gives no verdict that a drive circuit is adequate or safe. Every number here is your own entry.

Starting examples: a 5 V rail at 30% duty, a 300 µs on-time in a 1000 µs period, and an 8-bit counter on 5 V.

The average truth

A 5 V rail at 30% duty averages 1.5 V.

The duty truth

A 300 µs on-time in a 1000 µs period is 30% duty at 1000 Hz.

The steps truth

An 8-bit counter on a 5 V rail gives 256 steps of 0.39%, or 19.53 mV each.
